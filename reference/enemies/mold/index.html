Mold


Drone Enemies

Mold Host

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD HOST

- Classifying this as a "Drone"-type Mold variant
- Behavior: gets up close and personal; those big Mold clumps on its arms are used to clobber threats
- Regularly builds up and releases toxic spores around itself
- Spores are caustic; prolonged exposure causes severe lung damage and death
- Typically slow and ponderous in its movements
- Smells delicious (which I don't like to admit, but of all the Mold variants, this is the one I most want to take a bite out of, which skeeves me out but I can't deny it)
- Do not eat the Mold Host, obviously

 Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Mold Launcher

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD LAUNCHER

- A "Drone" type Mold variant
- Lobs Mold projectiles at threats (we FBC types qualify as "threats," so keep moving!)
- Projectiles fragment upon impact and release what lay folks would call a "puddle of disgusting goo" (essentially, a liquid but equally lethal version of the caustic spores released by the Host variant)
- Prefers high ground when possible (follow up with the Research people: a sign of intelligence, or just that famous Mold adaptation at work?)
- Relatively fragile, but will put up a fight if anyone gets too close
- (Kind of like me in my first decade of relationships)

Mold Spreader

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD SPREADER

- A resilient "Drone" type Mold variant
- Covered in hardened Mold growths in the front, but not in the back
- Produces spike-shaped, sharp Mold growths in its torso; launches them at nearby threats in a roughly cone-shaped pattern
- Takes a moment to charge up its spike launch attack!
- Completely lacks upper appendages (so at least it can't throw a punch)
- Instinctively (?) approaches and tracks threats, but moves slowly Kind of just... stumbles around and explodes barbs at people who get too close (possibly my mom at any family celebration)

Mold Spitter

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD SPITTER

- Another "Drone" type Mold variant
- Stationary (rooted?) when visible
- Can pull back into the ground, travel along an apparent hidden, rhizome-like Mold network, and reappear in another location
- Escapes if it senses a threat approaching
- Spits out spike-shaped Mold projectiles at anything it perceives as a threat
- Projectiles carry a potentially lethal Mold infection
- When killed (Destroyed? Is this stuff even properly alive?), leaves behind a toxic cloud

Heavy Enemies

Mold Blight

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD BLIGHT

- A "Heavy" type Mold variant
- Similar in appearance/behavior to the "Scrapper"
- Like the Scrapper: packs a mean punch at close range, fast, highly aggressive
- Unlike the Scrapper: unarmored, doesn't jump, doesn't spew toxic Mold (however, see "geyser," below!)
- Biohazard! Its appendages are covered with infectious Mold; don't let this one touch you
- At distance, the Mold Blight can deploy what I have very officially codenamed a "geyser" attack: it plunges its upper appendages into the ground and, after a few seconds, showers of toxic liquid erupt from the ground around its target
- Note: bubbling on the ground is a visual indication of the impending "geyser"
- The exact mechanism of this is unknown, but my working theory is that it entices or instructs an underground network of Mold to build up and release the liquid either that, or it actually creates an underground channel and pumps the goop through it?
- (This is all speculation; if somebody wants to try and get an evaluation out of Underhill, be my guest, I can't deal with that. Rick? You've got more patience than me)

 Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Mold Scrapper

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD SCRAPPER

- To no one's surprise, classifying this big fella as "Heavy"
- Punches the hell out of anything it can reach
- Goes after threats (faster than you'd think!)
- Jumps like a flea - except you don't want it to land on you, or even near you, trust me
- Also spews out a stream of toxic Mold; this is then repeated twice in short succession (note that the Scrapper takes a few seconds to build up pressure!)
- The Scrapper's front is covered with hardened Mold growths, which can take a beating, so deploying proper maneuvers/ordnance might make a difference if it comes to a fight

Mold Disperser

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD DISPERSER

- A huge "Heavy" type, clearly an offshoot of the "Mold Plague" variant
- (I say "clearly" as if I knew which one of them came first; obviously I don't)
- Slow, ponderous
- Like the Mold Plague, the Mold Disperser has committed itself to being a truly disgusting abomination: if a threat gets too close, it vomits and vomits and vomits and vomits and vomits - Several tubelike structures on its back launch projectiles
- The projectiles inflate in midair and become balloon-like floating objects, which behave like aerial mines; they burst open and leave a toxic cloud
- The tubelike structures are a point of vulnerability: if destroyed, they cause the Mold Disperser to lose effectiveness (Stunned? Disoriented? Not sure either term applies to something like the Mold)

Mold Plague

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD PLAGUE

- A huge "Heavy" type Mold variant
- Stomps around slowly
- Launches several Mold clump projectiles at threats from distance
- Upon impact, the projectiles burst open, resulting in caustic puddles of liquid Mold
- At close range, the Mold Plague exhibits truly gross behavior (trust me, you don't want to read the next sentence)
- If a threat gets too close, this nauseating blob of filth starts puking and pretty much doesn't stop: it just keeps coming and the puddle turns into a goddamn sea of lethal Mold vomit around it
- (Just typing this makes me want to take fifty showers)
- The Mold Plague's back carries a white Mold growth, apparently linked to something akin to a nervous system; reportedly, its destruction renders the Mold Plague much more vulnerable

Spore Mother

THREAT ASSESSMENT: SPORE MOTHER

- Falls under the "Heavy" category of Mold variants
- A huge floating Mold blob in a spheroid shape, consisting of Mold matter and, apparently, Mold-infected humans
- (Unclear what makes it float - some kind of lighter-than-air gas, paranatural field manipulation, what? needs further research) Can fire projectiles in any direction without turning
- Consistently fires two salvos of projectiles at targets (standard Mold projectile behavior: crack open, spread toxic goop, murder us)
- When the Spore Mother dies, it releases more projectiles that fall straight down, so don't get caught under it!
- (For the record, I was not the one who named this one "mother," that was Rick. I went to therapy and did the work precisely so I wouldn't feel the urge to do that)

Bloomed Spore Mother

THREAT ASSESSMENT: BLOOMED SPORE MOTHER

- A resilient "Heavy" variant
- Huge floating lump that most field reports have described as "disgusting," and this assessor officially concurs
- "Minelayer"-type behavior: it excretes (ew) volatile lumps of Mold matter from its body and uses arm-like appendages to throw them
- Said Mold lumps stick to surfaces and detonate when a threat comes near (note the short delay between detection and detonation!)
- A crust (also ew) of hardened Mold essentially turns it into a floating tank

Apex Enemies

Mold Blight - Apex

THREAT ASSESSMENT: MOLD BLIGHT APEX

- An "Apex" variant of the Mold Blight
- Unlike the normal Mold Blight, the Apex variant can leap around like its "cousin," the Scrapper
- When it lands, it causes "geysers" (pockets of high-pressure Mold liquid that spray up from the ground; see the baseline Mold Blight assessment for more disgusting details) to erupt around the landing site
- Notably hostile: when damaged, it deploys its "geysers" at a considerably more aggressive pace than the baseline variant
- Unfortunately, its aim also improves once it's angered; the "geysers" erupt directly under the target
- I say "angered," but there's no indication that the Mold in general, or this expression of the Mold in particular, has any emotional reaction to threats, or anything else, which I will admit does make me a little bit envious
- (Not to a degree where you need to report me to anybody, Ricky, you get worried way too easily. I'm okay, all things considered. Anyway, not all emotions are bad, some good ones sneak in too. especially when I beat you at chess. Want a rematch, by the way?)

 Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team
