Drone Enemies

Hedron Spike

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON SPIKE

-

A "Drone"-class Hedron variant

-

A Hedron-corrupted (coerced? co-opted? copied?) version of an Astral Spike

-

(Background: Astral Spikes are believed to be native inhabitants of the Astral Plane; see ██████████ for more information)

-

Native Astral Spikes are notoriously resistant to damage to the point of invulnerability: luckily, the Hedron Spike variant is much squishier

-

The Hedron Spike attempts to approach targets; once close enough, dashes towards the target

-

Visual identification is easy: if it looks like a migraine, you found one

-

(You're lucky you don't get migraines, Ricky)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Base

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON BASE

-

A "Drone"-class Hedron variant

-

Essentially, a duplicate of the Hedron Flux variant, except some of its coordinates have been transposed

-

Or to put it simply: whereas the Hedron Flux spins along its vertical axis, the Base variant spins along its horizontal axis

-

Speculative documentation from Research indicates that all Base variants and all Flux variants may simply (and I use the word loosely) be literally the same individual that occupies multiple positions in space simultaneously

-

(You may be wondering how that could be, given that they get destroyed at different times? Apparently they may also exist in multiple, recursive points in time!)

-

(So... that's cleared that up nicely)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Flux

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON FLUX

-

A "Drone"-class Hedron variant

-

Appears as a collection of smaller objects, fused (?) together into a shifting, recursive/geometric shape

-

Capable of spatial displacement ("teleportation"); actively approaches threats

-

At medium range, the Hedron Flux shifts its configuration into a vertical ring and advances towards the threat

-

Upon reaching its target, the Hedron Flux shifts into a semicircle and performs repetitive spinning movements along its vertical axis

-

According to Research, the identical objects that make up its body may, in fact, just be single instances of the object in question, occupying multiple positions in space simultaneously (...I'm not sure what to do with that information)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

-

A "Heavy"-class Hedron variant

-

Physically, the Hedron Axiom variant is built out of a person (male, Caucasian, early middle age, apparently hairless, as yet unidentified), presenting iteratively throughout the Axiom's "body"

Hedron Aggregate

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON AGGREGATE

-

A "Heavy"-class Hedron variant

-

Sudden rhythmic movements (possibly a part of larger pattern unclear)

-

Launches parts of itself as projectiles at targets

-

Projectiles explode at point of impact

-

If target gets too close, it performs a "stomp-like" attack to protect itself

-

Consists of a group of objects (or, again, a single object appearing in multiple points of spacetime?); namely, traffic lights

-

Why traffic lights? Why anything? (Not trying to be flippant; we really don't understand these things)

Hedron Sequence

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON SEQUENCE

-

Classification: Heavy

-

Presents as a torus-shaped "swarm" of objects

-

Maneuverable, defies gravity, attempts to maintain visual contact with threats

-

("Visual contact" is somewhat misleading given that they have no eyes and we have no idea how they sense anything - "line of sight" would probably be more accurate, but it's not like we even know that they have sight, so... ignore me)

-

Fires medium-speed projectiles in groups of three

-

Projectiles aren't ballistic; rather, they pursue targets

-

Speculation: given the self-targeting/gravity-defying nature of the projectiles, is it possible they are actually appendage-like parts of the torus despite their (apparent?) physical separation from the Hedron Sequence?

-

If so, then the Hedron Sequence isn't "firing" anything, it's reaching out to touch targets

-

(...very creepy)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Vector

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON VECTOR

-

Classification: Heavy

-

Visually resembles a brick wall pulled together into a toroid-like shape

-

Flight-capable in a way bricks really aren't supposed to be; charges rapidly at threats

-

Releases a wave of "force" (field reports are as of yet inconclusive; tentatively a wave of kinetic energy?) at the end of the charge

-

After performing the charge, the Hedron Vector becomes immobile (resting? recharging?).

-

This passive state is short-lived, but does render the Hedron Vector vulnerable

-

(For anyone unfortunate enough to end up fighting one of these things, I hereby officially endorse a "kick 'em while they're down" policy; you're welcome)

Apex Enemies

Hedron Axiom - Apex

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON AXIOM - APEX

-

"Apex"-class Hedron variant, evidently based on (or a fork of?) both the Aggregate Apex and Axiom variants?

-

As usual, invulnerable to direct assault

-

Follows a three-state behavioral pattern as it takes damage:

-

STATE 1

-

Flux and Base variants surround the Apex; damage to them is reflected onto the Apex itself

-

Fires a beam at one of the Flux/Base variants (chosen at random?), which is then refracted into multiple beams

-

STATE 2

-

The Apex becomes passive temporarily

-

The Flux/Base variants detach and commence firing bidirectional beams

-

The smaller variants soon return to the Apex, and a new, more aggressive cycle of attacks begins

-

STATE 3

-

Flux/Base variants again detach and fire independently at threats

-

However, the Axiom Apex itself now starts launching "energy discs" (best name I've got, sorry)

-

Once enough Flux/Base variants are destroyed, the Axiom Apex becomes faltered

-

(By the way, Rick, I know you prefer firsthand observations, and I get it, but some of these things are getting really dangerous. Just don't get stupid about it, okay?)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Aggregate - Apex

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON AGGREGATE - APEX

-

Hooo boy, where to even start with this one! Intel's spotty: as is often the case, much of this comes from interviews with Dylan Faden (see ██████████)

-

An "Apex" version of the Hedron Aggregate variant?

-

Unaffected by gravity, immobile, impervious to direct harm

-

Because nothing can ever be simple, this thing operates in three states:

-

STATE 1

-

Launches great numbers of projectiles in specific patterns (rows, columns, diamonds)

-

Surrounded by Flux and Base variants in "satellite formation"

-

Damage to the Flux and Base variants is reflected on (passed on to?) the Aggregate Apex itself

-

STATE 2

-

Once the variant suffers enough damage, it becomes passive. and the Flux/Base variants detach and pursue threats

-

They soon return to the Aggregate Apex, and a new, more complex cycle of movement and projectiles begins

-

STATE 3

-

Flux/Base variants again detach and pursue target

-

Aggregate Apex itself now remains active and continues to send out projectiles

-

Once enough Flux/Base variants are destroyed, the Apex becomes faltered

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Sequence - Apex

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON SEQUENCE - APEX

-

The Hedron Sequence "Apex" is a Hedron variant consisting of swarms of normal Hedron Sequence variants

-

As other Hedron Apex variants, it is invulnerable to direct assault, and it follows a three-state behavior pattern

-

Consists of two distinct groups of Hedron Sequence variants: the "core" group and the "satellite" group

-

STATE 1

-

The satellite group detaches from the core and flies out to attack threats with homing projectiles

-

The core group suffers damage when the satellite group is attacked

-

STATE 2

-

After enough damage has been inflicted, the core recalls the satellites, and attacks by manifesting (teleporting?) small, exploding objects near the target

-

The core also manifests Flux variants

-

Resumes its prior behavior, albeit much more aggressively

-

STATE 3

-

After suffering more damage, again behaves as above, except threats appear at an increased rate

-

Once enough satellites have been destroyed, the Sequence Apex becomes faltered

-

(Hey Ricky, this is out of left field, but if we actually survive this, wanna grab a coffee sometime?)

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team

Hedron Vector - Apex

THREAT ASSESSMENT: HEDRON VECTOR - APEX



-

Another "Apex"-class variant built out of swarms of Vector variants

-

Invulnerable to direct assault, three states of behavior

-

Has two elements: a "core" group and a "satellite" group, both made up of Hedron Vector variants.

-

STATE 1

-

At regular intervals, the satellite group detaches and makes independent attack runs

-

Any damage suffered by the satellite group is reflected onto the core group

-

STATE 2

-

Once the core is damaged enough, the satellites spread out and periodically initiate a "black hole" sequence, pulling threats in, causing injury via an "impulse wave"

-

Returns to its previous routine, albeit with more aggression and more "black hole" activations

-

(Side note: Research is debating if the "black hole" effect is an actual black hole. Disturbing that it's even a question)

-

STATE 3

-

After further core damage, the satellites separate and engage in intense "black hole" activity as before, but now the core also releases Flux variants

-

At this stage, the Apex can be made to falter by destroying enough of the satellite units

Lydia M.B. McPierce
Senior Analyst, Threat Assessment Team
